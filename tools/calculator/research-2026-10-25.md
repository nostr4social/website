## Notes by topic

### Egress (assumption `egress_gb`)
Nobody serves media from origin. CloudFront's own tiers fall from 0.085 to 0.020 a GB; Bunny's volume
tier is 0.005 or less; R2 egress is zero. A 50k-user media feed at 3 GB a user a month serves 1.8 PB a
year, which on CloudFront is the 0.025 to 0.030 tier and on Bunny under 0.005. Typical 0.02 is a large CDN
at volume; the high, 0.09, is AWS direct to the internet (the version-1 typical). The low, 0, is R2.

### DSAR (`dsar_rate_per_mau_year`, `dsar_cost`)
DataGrail's benchmarks are the only per-user volumes published: 859 per million identities in 2023 and
1,215 per 5 million monthly uniques in 2024 (its own customers, who bought privacy tooling, so both lean
high). Typical 0.0002 is the 2024 visitor figure rounded down; high 0.001 is the 2023 identity figure
rounded up. The 3.2 per thousand and the 1,524 a request are enterprise, manual-handling numbers and are
no longer used. No independent figure exists for an automated consumer export; 25 a request is an
unsourced allowance for a support touch plus an export job, 150 the high for a quarter-hour of an
engineer plus review.

### Breach (`breach_prob_year`)
IRIS 2025 is the first per-firm annual probability found: 9.3% for a typical firm in 2024, with firms
under 10M revenue at 0.60× the overall median (5.6%) and the information sector at 8.4%. The model's 5%
typical now rests on it. Cross-check from the insurance side: a 1,552 small-business policy at the 53%
industry loss ratio implies about 820 a year of expected payout per policy, far below the row's expected
loss, because the policy pays only part of a loss and most small firms carry 1M limits. The row is
therefore shown as risk exposure, apart from cash costs. IRIS median loss for a firm under 100M revenue
(357K) is about a third of the model's severity at 50k users (min of the cap and 20 a user), which was
not changed; it is the overridable number.

### Relays (`relay_per_mau_year`, `relay_floor_year`)
No operator publishes cost per user. Hosted relays run 10 to 20 a month; one operator's rule of thumb
is a thousand active users per vCPU and 2 GB (about 12 a month on a European VPS), which is 0.15 a user
a year all-in before strfry's efficiencies. Typical 0.05 a user a year with a 600 a year floor (one
managed relay with backups) is what the market will bear, marked as Nostr4's thesis.

### Jurisdiction weights (`jur_weight_*`)
The form asks which regions an app has users in, not how many in each. Weights are social media users:
US 254M and UK 55.5M (DataReportal, October 2025); EU about 230M (67% of roughly 340M aged 16 to 74,
Eurostat); other set equal to the US on purpose, since the rest of the world is too large to weight by
size. The real split is a question for the form.

### California share (`ca_share_of_us_users`)
Census Vintage 2025: 39.36M of 341.8M, 11.5%. The CCPA threshold of 100k California consumers is now
compared with US users times this share, so a US-only app crosses it at about 870k users rather than
100k.

### US verification share (`verify_share_us`)
No platform-by-platform share of users sent to a paid check exists. The practice evidence points one
way: estimation on suspected minors (Meta, YouTube), document checks only where a law requires them
(Reddit), and the Texas and Utah laws placing the age signal at the app store. Typical 2%, high 10%.

### Payments (notes only)
Apple's rules still restrict bypassing in-app purchase for digital goods inside the iOS app outside the
EU (DMA terms), Japan (MSCA, since 2025-12-18) and the US link-out ruling (commission barred pending the
Supreme Court's contempt review, No. 25-1311). On-ramp costs on peer-to-peer rails are borne by users and
affect conversion, not the app's P&L. No assumption changed.

## Looked for and not found

1. CloudFront tiers between 50 TB and 1 PB from third-party summaries disagree; the official
   pay-as-you-go page was used instead.
2. A Cloudflare statement that CDN plans include unmetered bandwidth: the plans page makes no such claim,
   so Cloudflare's CDN is not used as the low (R2's zero is).
3. An automated cost per DSAR from anyone who does not sell DSAR automation.
4. A 2025 or 2026 DataGrail per-million figure beyond the 2024 webinar benchmark.
5. Per-firm annual breach probability for firms under 10M revenue as an absolute figure: IRIS gives the
   overall figure and a relative multiplier, not the tier's own curve values (Figure 7 is an image).
6. A standalone-cyber loss ratio for 2025 (the 53% is industry-wide).
7. Cost per active user of any production Nostr relay (Damus, nostr.wine, Primal publish nothing).
8. An EU-wide social media user count in people (derived from Eurostat's share and population instead).
9. The share of US users any social platform sends to a paid identity or age check.
10. A UK CMA decision on Apple's steering terms.
11. Utah SB 142's enforcement date from the enacted code itself (secondary sources conflict).
